Legal Background

The boundaries of legality

The difference between an ethical hacker and a cybercriminal lies not in the tools used, but in legal authorization. The same activity — e.g. a port scan — can be legal with a contract and a criminal offense without one.

Even an attempt can be an offense — not just a successful intrusion.

Budapest Convention on Cybercrime

This treaty forms the basis for global cybercrime regulation. It uniformly defines unauthorized access, data manipulation, and disruption of system operations.

If a technique is prohibited here, it is very likely present in the laws of most countries.

CFAA – Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (USA)

One of the most important reference laws. It emphasizes "exceeding authorized access" — even the way you use a system you have basic access to matters.

In the USA, even a port scan can be a grey area with malicious intent.

Rules of Engagement (RoE)

Every professional pentest starts with a written contract. Required elements:

  • Scope – which IPs and domains are in scope
  • Tools – e.g., whether DoS testing is prohibited
  • Schedule – when testing may be performed
  • Communication – who to notify in case of an incident

GDPR and data protection

If you access personal data (PII) during testing, EU GDPR applies. The penalty is not imprisonment but massive fines.

During pentesting: mandatory anonymization and secure handling of data.
