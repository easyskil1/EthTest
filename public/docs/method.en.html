Pentest Methodology

Pre-engagement – Preparation

Establishing the "rules of the game". The test objective, scope (which IPs are in range), permitted techniques, timeframe, and communication channel for incidents are defined.

This phase legally protects both the client and the tester.

Intelligence Gathering – OSINT

Passive data collection — no direct contact with the target system:

  • Public databases, social media
  • Domain information (WHOIS, DNS MX/TXT)
  • Leaked password databases

This step can reveal a surprisingly large amount of critical information.

Threat Modeling

Building strategy from collected data. Questions: where is valuable data stored, which systems are critical to the business, what attack surfaces are accessible from the outside?

The goal: don't try randomly, build targeted attack paths.

Vulnerability Analysis

Automated scanning: searching for known CVEs, misconfigurations, and outdated software.

Manual analysis (more valuable): logical flaws, business process weaknesses, unique exploit opportunities.

Automated tools are fast but often produce false positives. Manual review refines this.

Exploitation

Actually exploiting discovered vulnerabilities. Typical techniques:

  • Password cracking (brute-force, dictionary)
  • SQL injection
  • XSS (Cross-Site Scripting)
  • Service exploitation
Goal is proof, not destruction — only go as far as necessary.

Post-Exploitation and Reporting

Demonstrating the business impact of the test: privilege escalation, lateral movement, searching for sensitive data.

The report contains a list of vulnerabilities with risk classification (critical/high/medium/low), evidence, and concrete remediation recommendations.
